CASE 03 · RISK & GOVERNANCE

A 50% readiness score conceals the actual exposure.

A fictional organization’s personal-data inventory becomes a prioritized control backlog. The point is to test obligations and ownership, not to declare compliance with a single score.

context

The situation

A growing learning-platform provider handles data for customers, employees, marketing prospects and support contacts. Business owners know that privacy obligations matter, but have no single operational picture of where personal data is collected, why it is processed, what notice or lawful basis applies, who can access it, and when it should be deleted.

The management challenge is to turn a broad mandate into traceable action by processing activity, control owner and deadline rather than produce a reassuring aggregate heat map.

Decision question. Which control gaps should be fixed first, who is accountable and what evidence will demonstrate closure?

diagnosis

The diagnosis

A sample rubric scores five fictional processing activities, then groups missing controls by priority. The composite result is 50%, but the distribution matters far more than the average.

The 23% marketing score should trigger targeted review of consent or other lawful processing grounds, notice, data-sharing, retention and withdrawal journeys. But the number does not itself establish a legal violation; it only prioritizes deeper evidence collection.

intervention

The governance intervention

Convert data inventory to a control-evidence system. Each processing activity should connect purpose, data category, data principal, actor, processor, retention, sharing, security controls, lawful ground, complaints handling, and accountable owner.

  • Inventory: map the process rather than count applications; one system can support several processing purposes.
  • Obligation mapping: have qualified legal and compliance teams determine applicability, transitional timing, and evidence required.
  • Gap register: give each failed control an owner, remediation action, dependency, target and residual risk decision.
  • Independent closure: retest with evidence such as a working deletion request, access review, contract, log or incident drill.

findings

What the sample actually shows

The inventory reveals heterogeneity. Payroll sits at 86% under the fictional rubric while marketing and product journeys have substantially lower control evidence. An organization-wide average masks the critical paths and prevents effective remediation sequencing.

The scoring model can help organize a conversation, but only a traceable register links that conversation to testable control closure. A low score is a review signal, not a verdict; a high score is not legal safe harbor.

Readiness is a portfolio of documented obligations and working controls, not a single percentage.

decision

The management recommendation

Recommended next decision — not delivered compliance: assign executive ownership for all ten fictional priority-1 gaps; validate actual legal applicability; fund the high-dependency controls first; and schedule control retesting followed by a rehearsed incident drill.

limits

What this case does not prove

  • 50% is not a recognized regulator-issued DPDP compliance score.
  • Counts and classifications are invented; no real entity or data processing was assessed.
  • No legal conclusions, certification, or readiness completion are asserted.
  • Obligations and commencement dates should be verified against current official law and rules before implementation.
