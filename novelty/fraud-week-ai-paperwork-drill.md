# Fraud Week Five-Day Drill: AI-Made Paperwork

*A free novelty item from The Signal (readsigil.com). Available until Saturday 21 November 2026, 11:59 PM IST.*

International Fraud Awareness Week runs from 15 to 21 November 2026 (organised by the ACFE, with partner organisations around the world: https://www.acfe.com/fraudweek). Most Fraud Week material is about the classic red flags. This drill adds one modern twist for finance, procurement, audit, HR and operations teams: **the paperwork itself can now be made by AI.** Receipts, invoices, bank-detail change letters, approval emails, voice notes and even job applicants can be generated in minutes and look clean.

The drill is five short sessions, one per working day from Monday 16 to Friday 20 November. Each takes about ten minutes at a team stand-up or over lunch. Do them in order, or pick the ones that matter most to your desk. It is an awareness exercise, not a control framework. Your own fraud, audit, HR, risk and incident processes always come first.

## Ground rules

- Use only synthetic, redacted or already-public material. Never paste real invoices, receipts, employee records, bank details, credentials or customer data into a public AI tool.
- Do not generate convincing forgeries of real companies, real people's signatures or real bank letterheads. Describe what a fake would look like; don't make one.
- Treat every model output as a first draft. An AI model cannot tell you whether a document or voice is genuine. Verification happens through an independent channel you already trust.
- Nothing here is legal, regulatory, security or fraud advice, and it is not about any particular institution.

## Day 1 (Mon 16 Nov): The too-perfect receipt

**Scenario:** an expense claim arrives with a crisp, plausible restaurant receipt. Image generators can now produce receipts with realistic creases, logos and totals.

**Ten-minute exercise:** list what your expense process actually checks today (amount, date, merchant, card match, policy). Mark which checks a generated image would pass automatically.

**Prompt:**
> I review employee expense claims. Here is a synthetic description of our current checks: [paste]. List the checks that an AI-generated receipt image would pass without any human noticing, and suggest three independent checks (for example card-transaction matching or merchant look-up) that do not rely on how the receipt looks. Mark anything you are unsure of as "unknown".

**One question for the team:** do we ever match the receipt to the card or bank transaction, or only to the policy?

## Day 2 (Tue 17 Nov): The vendor who changed their bank account

**Scenario:** an email, on a real supplier's letterhead and in their usual tone, asks you to update their bank details before the next payment run. The tone and formatting can now be copied by a model from a few old emails.

**Ten-minute exercise:** walk through your bank-detail change process step by step. Where does a call-back happen, to which number, and who is allowed to skip it when a payment is urgent?

**Prompt:**
> Here is a synthetic version of our supplier bank-detail change process: [paste]. Red-team it. Assume the attacker can write emails and letters that perfectly match the supplier's style. List each step where a well-written request could get through, and rewrite the process so that verification always uses contact details we held before the request arrived.

**One question for the team:** if the request says "urgent, payment run is tomorrow", what exactly do we do?

## Day 3 (Wed 18 Nov): The voice note from the boss

**Scenario:** a short voice note or call, in a senior leader's voice, asks someone to approve a payment, share a file or bypass a step "just this once". Voice cloning needs only a short public clip.

**Ten-minute exercise:** agree a call-back rule for any unusual request that arrives by voice or video, however senior the person sounds, and decide who is allowed to say no.

**Prompt:**
> Draft a one-paragraph team rule for handling unusual payment or access requests that arrive by phone, voice note or video call, even from senior leaders. It must include: call back on a number from our own directory, never on one given in the request; a code word or second approver for high-value requests; and explicit permission to say no without penalty. Keep it under 120 words and in plain English.

**One question for the team:** has the most senior person on the team said out loud that they want to be called back?

## Day 4 (Thu 19 Nov): The applicant who isn't there

**Scenario:** a candidate with a polished CV interviews well on video, but the face, voice, documents or even the whole identity may be synthetic, or one person may be fronting for someone else. Remote hiring and contractor onboarding are the soft spots.

**Ten-minute exercise:** list every point in hiring and onboarding where identity is checked, and how (live, document, reference, device). Mark which ones happen only over video or email.

**Prompt:**
> Here is a synthetic outline of our hiring and contractor onboarding steps: [paste]. Identify where a synthetic or borrowed identity could pass, and suggest proportionate extra checks (for example a live document check, verified references reached through official channels, or watching for mismatches between the interviewee and the person who starts). Avoid suggestions that would treat candidates unfairly or collect more personal data than needed.

**One question for the team:** after day one, would we notice if the person doing the work was not the person we hired?

## Day 5 (Fri 20 Nov): Close the loop

**Ten-minute exercise:** pick the one gap from Days 1 to 4 that worries the team most. Write a one-line owner, a one-line fix and a date. Put it in your normal risk or ticket system, not in a chat thread.

**Prompt:**
> Here are our notes from four short fraud-awareness sessions on AI-generated receipts, bank-detail change requests, cloned voices and synthetic job applicants: [paste synthetic or redacted notes]. Summarise the three most important gaps in plain English, propose one owner-and-date action for each, and draft a five-line note to the team saying what we will change. Do not invent facts that are not in the notes.

## The five-minute checklist (any day)

- [ ] Every payment-detail change is verified using contact details we already held.
- [ ] Unusual voice or video requests get a call-back on a directory number, whoever they claim to be.
- [ ] Someone on the team has been told, explicitly, that saying no to an urgent senior request is fine.
- [ ] Expense receipts are matched to a transaction, not just to the policy.
- [ ] Remote hires and contractors have at least one identity check that isn't on a video call.
- [ ] The one gap we found has an owner and a date in our real tracking system.
