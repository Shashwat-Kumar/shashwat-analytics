AI governance

The policies, controls and accountabilities that decide what an AI system may do, who answers for it, and how its decisions can be explained and corrected. In practice it means drawing a line between actions a system can take on its own and actions that need a human checkpoint, a log and an owner.

Agentic AI in banking

Agentic AI describes systems that plan and take multi-step actions, such as calling tools, querying data or triggering workflows, rather than only answering questions. In banking and other regulated settings the hard questions are about authority: what each agent is permitted to do, spending and approval limits, identity, and an audit trail that a reviewer can follow.

Read: If agents can collaborate, they can trade · Faster cars need better brakes

Multi-agent systems

Architectures in which several specialised agents (search, write, check, plan) hand work to one another. Much of the behaviour, capability and risk lives in the interfaces and checks between agents rather than inside any single model.

Read: Intelligence without a main character

Goodhart's law

Often paraphrased as: when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure. People, and optimising algorithms, learn to move the number without moving the thing it was meant to reflect.

Read: When the score becomes the goal · Attention is not the same as value

Counter-metrics

Measures paired with a primary target to reveal gaming or unintended side effects, for example complaints alongside handling speed, or repeat contacts alongside closures. Treating metrics as a portfolio, and reviewing samples of real behaviour, keeps a scorecard honest.

Read: When the score becomes the goal

Data quality

Whether data is fit for the decision it supports: complete, consistent, correctly defined and traceable. Useful habits include a metric dictionary, automated checks on volumes, nulls and ranges at each pipeline step, named dataset owners and known-answer tests run on every refresh.

Read: Models are only as good as their inputs

Churn modelling and decile lift

Churn models score how likely each customer is to leave. A ranked list is usually judged by lift: how much more concentrated churners are in the top deciles than in a random sample. Comparing a complex model with a simple baseline shows whether the extra complexity earns its keep.

Read: Synthetic churn exploration · churn-propensity-model on GitHub

Credit risk scorecards

Credit scorecards estimate the probability that a borrower defaults. A classic approach bins variables, measures each one's predictive strength with weight of evidence (WoE) and information value (IV), fits a logistic regression and scales the result to points. Performance is commonly checked with AUC, KS, Gini and calibration.

DPDP readiness

Preparing for India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and the DPDP Rules, 2025: knowing where personal data sits, the purpose of each processing activity, notices and consent, processor contracts, retention and erasure, breach response and a channel for data principals' rights.
