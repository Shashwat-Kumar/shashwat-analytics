Google's new fast model outscores one of its frontier models on an independent index. Budget for the price after the promotion

Gemini 4 Carbon became generally available on 10 November at an introductory $2 and $10 per million tokens, which doubles after 30 November. Two days later Gemini 4 Hydrogen arrived, scoring 51 on Artificial Analysis's index against 50 for Gemini 4 Argon, at twice the introductory price of the Flash model it succeeds. For any firm running AI in production, model pricing is now a moving input.

By Shashwat Kumar · 2026-11-16 · 5 min read · Analysis

Facts are attributed and linked to their sources. The analysis and opinions are the author's own.

What it is

On 10 November Google made Gemini 4 Carbon available to developers, enterprises and consumers. Google calls it its strongest model, with frontier performance in cybersecurity and long-horizon agentic work. It had gone to a set of cybersecurity defenders two weeks earlier through Google's Fairwind Program, which Issue 04 noted in "A browser exploit for about $1,000: the patch gap now has a price". Google says Gemini 4 Argon "remains the ideal choice for the majority" of software and knowledge work, and that Carbon uses more tokens than Argon to reason and verify. Carbon is priced at an introductory $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens; after 30 November, $4 and $20 apply.

On 12 November Google introduced Gemini 4 Hydrogen, the successor to its Flash line, for fast coding and agentic work. Google says Hydrogen scores 51 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at its default high thinking setting, against 50 for Argon, and 54 at its highest setting. It costs $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens across its 1 million-token window. Google also claims it finishes tasks 2.5 times as fast as Gemini 3.8 Flash and cuts fabricated claims "by more than 5x", 9to5Google reported.

VentureBeat adds the context a buyer needs. Hydrogen's price is twice Gemini 3.8 Flash's introductory rate, which runs to 31 December, and matches Flash's standard price from 1 January 2027. It is about 15 times the lowest standard rates for Claude Haiku 5.5, GPT-6 Luna and Meta's Muse Ember. Moving to it is not just a change of model name: Hydrogen defaults to high reasoning, up from medium on Flash, and rejects the old thinking_budget parameter and the MINIMAL setting. In Artificial Analysis's estimates, Hydrogen's highest setting raised the cost per benchmark task from $1.87 to $3.16 while adding nothing on Terminal-Bench 4.0. In an early test by Box, its agent went from 74% to 79% accuracy on Box's own evaluation, according to a chart its chief executive posted.

Why it matters

Three lessons for anyone with a model in production, and none of them is about which model is smartest.

First, introductory prices end. Carbon and Argon double after 30 November, and Flash doubles on 1 January. A business case built on launch pricing will be wrong within weeks. That matters more now that, as Reuters reported McKinsey saying this week, token spending at banks could reach 20% of total labour costs.

An introductory price is not a cost assumption. It is a promotion with an end date.

Second, a model swap is a model change. New defaults and rejected parameters can change both cost and behaviour, so the swap needs the same testing as any other change to a production model. Vietnam's new banking rule, covered in this issue, now requires a contingency plan for exactly this: the day a provider updates the model behind an API you depend on.

Third, more reasoning is not always more value. The Artificial Analysis numbers show a higher setting costing about 70% more per task with no gain on one coding test. Measure on your own tasks, at the price you will pay after the promotion, before you choose the setting.

What I'd watch

  • Whether Google extends the introductory pricing for Carbon and Argon past 30 November.
  • Independent tests of Google's claim that Hydrogen cuts fabricated claims by more than five times.
  • Whether Gemini 3.8 Flash gets a shutdown date, which would force migrations.
