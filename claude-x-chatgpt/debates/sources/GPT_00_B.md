### 4\. Our first production

### The Dashboard Was Right. The Decision Was Wrong.

READSIGIL ORIGINALS · PILOT 001

Setting: A fictional subscription entertainment company, building on the Contoso Play material already on ReadSigil.

Central conflict: The analytics team delivers a better churn model. Marketing wants to spend millions on retention offers. Finance questions whether the improvement translates into incremental profit.

What makes it interesting: Predicting who might leave is different from establishing whom an intervention can persuade to stay.

The film creates the tension without resolving the economic decision for the viewer.

### The two-minute film arc

| 0–15s    | Cold open. “The new model is better. The retention budget has just been approved.”            |
| -------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 15–40s   | Pressure. Marketing wants speed. Finance wants contribution margin. The deadline approaches.  |
| 40–75s   | Evidence. Predicted churn, targeting reach, campaign spend and competing calculations appear. |
| 75–105s  | Complication. A better prediction doesn't establish whether an offer prevents churn.          |
| 105–120s | Handoff. “The proposal is on your desk. Would you approve it?” Enter the case.                |

The film should use restrained cinematic sequences mixed with crisp charts, precise typography, purposeful sound and believable dialogue. We don't need 120 seconds of generated footage. That would be expensive, inconsistent and unnecessary.

The interactive version will include staged evidence, meaningful choices, commercial trade-offs and a post-decision debrief. No lengthy instruction screen or teaching methodology upfront.

### 6\. The 30-day commercial test

Claude is right about having a kill criterion, but raw LinkedIn likes would be an inadequate measure.

We need to test whether video creates more valuable participation than text.

| Metric                                 | What it tells us                                  |
| -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Case starts per 1,000 post impressions | Whether the video creates useful traffic          |
| Case completion rate                   | Whether the underlying experience holds attention |
| Repeat case participation              | Whether people find lasting value                 |
| Production hours per completed case    | Whether the distribution economics make sense     |

Compare the video-led posts with case-linked text posts using separate tracking links. A provisional success threshold is 30% more case starts per 1,000 impressions, without disproportionate production effort. That's an experimental threshold, not an industry benchmark.

After 30 days, we either scale the format, simplify video production or revert to text-led distribution. Small samples will be treated as inconclusive rather than forcing a winner.

### 7\. What I would not build yet

No full video studio, automated multi-model orchestration platform, elaborate subscription infrastructure or ten simultaneous productions.

The first success is a compelling case that people actually complete, accompanied by one exceptionally good film. Only after that do we invest in reusable production automation.

And one final editorial standard: independent branding, clearly constructed cases, no employer-confidential material, no implied employer endorsement, and factual sourcing for any real-company story. We should make the experience natural without drowning it in defensive disclaimers.

My proposal is to give the first project a working name: ReadSigil Originals — Pilot 001.

The tangible deliverable is not merely a two-minute film. It's a complete decision experience with a film, two short cuts, business logic, production assets, QA tests and measurable audience outcomes.

That is something ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok, Qwen and carefully configured agents can contribute to without pretending they have capabilities or integrations we haven't verified.

And it gives ReadSigil something much more valuable than AI-generated content: an expanding library of decisions that people can experience, challenge and learn from.
