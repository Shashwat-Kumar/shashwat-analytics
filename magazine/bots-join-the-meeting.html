AI is now a participant in the meeting. Check who it lets in, and what it keeps

Anthropic's Claude Meetings and xAI's Grok Bot can now join video calls, answer questions and start work while people talk. Their design choices differ in ways a compliance team should read before anyone turns on auto-join.

By Shashwat Kumar · 2026-11-17 · 5 min read · Analysis

Facts are attributed and linked to their sources. The analysis and opinions are the author's own.

What it is

On 10 November Anthropic announced Claude Meetings, in beta on Team and Enterprise plans and on Google Meet only for now. Claude joins as a visible participant and posts a notice in the call's chat. It can draft documents and whiteboards while people talk and answer questions in the chat. It saves a transcript, a summary and screen descriptions, but not audio. By default it stays out of calls when someone from outside the organisation is invited, and leaves if an outsider joins partway through; an admin can change that, in which case Claude posts its notice but does not reply in the chat, and guests' words are included in the notes. It uses each person's connected tools with that person's permission, asking the first time; when someone asks out loud, it confirms each time who is speaking. During the beta it is not available to organisations with zero data retention, HIPAA-ready configuration or customer-managed encryption keys. Anthropic tells customers to make sure everyone on their calls is OK with being transcribed.

xAI's Grok Bot changelog for version 1.0.1, dated 11 November and including 1.0.0, says a bot on Team, Enterprise or Ultra plans can join a Google Meet, Zoom or Microsoft Teams call from a link, answer in the meeting chat and send a summary afterwards. Adding a team bot to Microsoft Teams needs a team admin to turn it on, an owner or manager to connect it and a Teams admin to approve it. What teammates teach a team bot waits under "Review learnings" until the owner or a manager approves it. Owners and managers can share their own app login as a shared connector, once a team admin turns on "Allow Shared Logins". The launch post says bots respect each person's permissions, ask before sensitive actions and check whether information is appropriate to share in a public channel.

Why it matters

A meeting is where decisions are made and where things are said that never reach email. Putting an AI into it raises the same questions as any recording system, plus new ones, because this participant can act. I would ask five questions of any meeting bot. Does it join calls with clients or other outsiders, and who decides? Does it keep audio, or only text? When someone asks it out loud to fetch something, whose permissions does it use, and how does it know who spoke? If it acts through a shared login, does the audit trail show who actually asked? And who approves what it learns and remembers?

A bot that remembers what the team taught it is building institutional knowledge. Someone should approve what goes in.

The two products answer these differently, and both include controls worth copying. Claude's default of leaving when an outsider joins is the right one for a bank, where client calls carry confidentiality duties. Grok Bot's review gate for learnings treats the bot's memory as something to be approved, not just accumulated. Shared logins are convenient, but in a regulated firm an action taken under one person's credentials at another's request is exactly what auditors look for. Claude Meetings' beta exclusion of zero-retention and customer-key organisations also means the most tightly controlled firms cannot use it yet. In my view, firms should write a short meeting-bot policy now: consent and notice, retention, external calls, and who may turn on auto-join, before teams switch these features on themselves.

What I'd watch

  • What Microsoft announces for meetings and agents at Ignite, which runs from 17 to 20 November.
  • Whether Claude Meetings opens to zero-retention and customer-managed-key organisations after the beta.
  • Whether meeting bots add audit records showing who asked for each action, not only what was done.
