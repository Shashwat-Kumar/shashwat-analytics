### Banking Problem Atlas — 100-Bank Opportunity Study

This is the right direction. We should stop looking for products for a moment and build a comprehensive map of everything that can go wrong, cost money, create risk, delay growth or frustrate customers inside a bank.

Then we can discover which problems are widespread, financially material, poorly solved, and have a budget owner willing to pay.

One distinction matters: covering 100 banks is not the same as establishing that all 100 have a particular problem. We'll create a universal problem taxonomy and a separate bank-by-bank evidence matrix. Unknowns remain unknown until verified.

### 1\. The master problem matrix

I would organize the banking universe into six economic outcomes.

Earn more

Acquisition, deposits, lending, pricing, distribution, cross-sell

Spend less

Processes, reconciliation, workforce, vendors, technology costs

Lose less

Fraud, credit losses, leakage, disputes, security incidents

Stay compliant

Regulatory obligations, controls, audit, privacy, governance

Retain trust

Service, complaints, reliability, transparency, customer outcomes

Use capital better

Liquidity, funding, capital allocation, balance sheet, treasury

Within these, I propose 25 banking domains spanning front office, middle office, back office and enterprise infrastructure.

This broad structure aligns with internationally recognized banking supervisory areas such as credit risk, liquidity, operational resilience, internal controls and financial-services abuse. It is broader than the supervisory taxonomy because we're also looking for commercial and operating problems.

You're right. My earlier matrix was too technology- and process-centric. It looked like a map of banking functions, not a complete map of the problems involved in running a bank.

I had underrepresented some of the most consequential areas: employee attrition, leadership quality, succession, compensation, organizational productivity, capital constraints, ESG, culture and execution capability.

In fact, a bank can have excellent technology and still destroy shareholder value through weak people management, poor capital allocation or ineffective leadership.

### We need a broader problem universe

I'd expand the Banking Problem Atlas into 10 problem families, cutting across every function.

| Family                                | Problems we must capture                                                                   |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1. Growth & market                    | Market share, acquisition, competitive threats, product relevance, distribution            |
| 2. Customers & experience             | Churn, trust, complaints, service accessibility, financial inclusion                       |
| 3. People & organization              | Attrition, vacancies, succession, leadership, incentives, workforce capability             |
| 4. Productivity & execution           | Output per employee, branch productivity, bureaucracy, project failure, idle capacity      |
| 5. Financial performance              | Profitability, cost efficiency, pricing, revenue leakage, shareholder returns              |
| 6. Capital, liquidity & balance sheet | Capital adequacy, RWA, ROE, deposit funding, ALM, concentration                            |
| 7. Credit, fraud & enterprise risk    | Delinquencies, NPAs, fraud, operational losses, model failures, resilience                 |
| 8. Technology, data & AI              | Legacy systems, outages, cyber risk, modernization, data reliability, AI controls          |
| 9. Governance, regulation & conduct   | Board effectiveness, audit, regulatory compliance, ethics, customer fairness               |
| 10. ESG & long-term sustainability    | Climate exposure, financed emissions, physical risk, social impact, governance, disclosure |

And these families need to be examined through every level of the institution: Board, MD/CEO, business units, regions, branches, teams, employees, customers, processes and individual transactions.

For example, attrition isn't merely an HR problem. It can increase acquisition costs, reduce relationship continuity, damage service quality and weaken collections. Similarly, capital constraints affect lending growth, pricing, product strategy and the bank's valuation.

That's the level of connectedness the matrix needs.

### Four examples of how deep we should go

| Problem            | Questions that uncover commercial value                                                                                          |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Employee attrition | What does one regrettable exit cost in recruitment, training, lost productivity and lost customer relationships?                 |
| Capital efficiency | How much capital is tied up in low-return assets? What is the effect of improving risk-adjusted returns?                         |
| Productivity       | Why do comparable branches with similar customer opportunities deliver radically different output per employee?                  |
| ESG & climate risk | Which loan portfolios, collateral pools and physical locations have material climate exposure that existing risk processes miss? |

And here's the next improvement I would make to the research method: every problem should be examined from three perspectives simultaneously—what the Board worries about, what management struggles to execute, and what frontline employees or customers experience.

That will reveal problems that conventional technology-vendor research misses.

The 246 rows are a starting taxonomy, not a claim of exhaustive coverage. We should expand and challenge it as we analyze different bank types.

Most importantly, we should not rank opportunities purely by size of the problem. A ₹1,000 crore banking problem with no accessible buyer can be less commercially attractive than a ₹10 crore problem with a clear owner, measurable financial impact and a short purchasing cycle.

That's how we'll move from all the problems banks have to the problems banks are genuinely desperate to pay someone to solve.
