Meta's chip suppliers are now buying its shares

Meta plans to raise $95 billion, and NVIDIA and AMD are putting $4 billion of it in. Anthropic, meanwhile, warned that some of its compute partners depend on borrowed money. The AI build-out's funding is becoming a loop that credit teams need to map.

By Shashwat Kumar · 2026-11-03 · 4 min read · Analysis

Facts are attributed and linked to their sources. The analysis and opinions are the author's own.

What it is

On 27 October Meta said it plans to raise $95 billion: $35 billion through underwritten equity offerings and a $60 billion at-the-market programme, CNBC reported. NVIDIA is investing $3 billion and AMD $1 billion. In a release dated 26 October, NVIDIA said Meta will expand its use of Vera Rubin systems and Vera CPUs, with Spectrum-X networking and BlueField-4, and that the two are considering work on NVIDIA's OpenShell agent-security architecture. CNBC also reported that AMD's Helios racks are running at Meta and that EPYC processors power Muse.

Citing Dealroom, CNBC said NVIDIA has taken part in 121 investment rounds so far this year, against 13 in 2022, and AMD in 25, against 2. In July AMD committed up to $5 billion to Anthropic. A week later, Anthropic's amended S-1 said some of its compute providers "depend on third-party financing or credit support to acquire chips and develop capacity".

Why it matters

In my view this changes how you read demand. When a chipmaker buys equity in a customer that is buying its chips, the purchase order is no longer a clean signal. The same few companies now appear as supplier, investor and customer, sometimes in the same deal. That is not improper. Vendor financing is an old pattern in capital-intensive industries. But it ties the risks together: if one part of the loop slows, the others feel it.

When the supplier is also the shareholder, a purchase order is no longer a clean signal of demand.

For lenders to data-centre developers, chip-backed borrowers and the cloud providers that rent capacity, I would map the chain explicitly: who supplies whom, who funds whom, and who has committed to buy capacity from whom. Concentration limits that treat each name separately will understate the exposure if the names are this connected.

What I'd watch

  • How fast Meta draws on its $60 billion at-the-market programme.
  • Whether more chip suppliers take equity in their largest customers.
  • Anthropic's roadshow, and whether the valuation discussed by investors moves again.
