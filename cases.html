READSIGIL / THE CASEBOOK

The decision.
Not the dashboard.

Cases built around a management question: what was broken, why, which options were rejected, what changed and how to tell whether it worked.

What makes a case credible? An explicit baseline. An intervention. A counterfactual. A business owner. An outcome that can be checked. Anything less is a demonstration, not a success story.

HOW TO READ THESE CASES

Business problem → decision → evidence → outcome.

Inspired by the problem-led and impact-led structures used in McKinsey and BCG client stories, and the operational detail in technology customer stories. These are original analyses, not consulting-firm engagements.

CONTEXT & STAKES

Who owns the decision, what is changing, and what economic value is at risk?

BASELINE & DIAGNOSIS

What is the starting performance, its denominator and root causes? Which hypotheses survive the evidence?

OPTIONS & TRADE-OFFS

What are the realistic alternatives, including doing nothing? What does each cost?

INTERVENTION DESIGN

What changes in people, process, data and technology? Who is accountable?

RESULTS & ATTRIBUTION

What is actually observed, what is estimated, and what needs a controlled pilot?

LIMITATIONS & TRANSFER

What could invalidate the finding, and what can a reader use elsewhere?

03 / EDITORIAL METHOD

The seven questions behind every case.

01 · What was the decision?

The choice management actually controlled, not the technology selected.

02 · What was the baseline?

Numbers, definition, population and period before intervention.

03 · What explained the gap?

Competing hypotheses and the evidence that distinguishes them.

04 · What alternatives existed?

Why doing nothing, a simpler fix or a different tool might win.

05 · How would it ship?

Owner, cadence, controls, dependencies and rollout sequence.

06 · What outcome counts?

Financial contribution, cost, risk, counterfactual and attribution.

07 · What would kill it?

Pre-agreed stop thresholds, caveats and transferable lesson.
