READSIGIL / THE CASEBOOK

Cases in analytics
and the decisions behind them

Each case places the reader in the position of an executive with a decision to make, sets out the evidence available at the time, and ends with a recommendation and the test that would show whether it worked.

Standard of evidence. A baseline, an intervention, a counterfactual, a named owner and an outcome that can be checked.

HOW TO READ THESE CASES

Each case follows the same sequence

The six stages below appear in every case, in the same order, so that cases can be read side by side.

CONTEXT & STAKES

Who owns the decision, what has changed, and what value is at stake?

BASELINE & DIAGNOSIS

What is current performance, measured on what base, and which explanations survive the evidence?

OPTIONS & TRADE-OFFS

What are the realistic alternatives, including doing nothing, and what does each cost?

INTERVENTION DESIGN

What changes in people, process, data and technology, and who is accountable?

RESULTS & ATTRIBUTION

What has been observed, what is estimated, and what still needs a controlled test?

LIMITATIONS & TRANSFER

What could overturn the finding, and what applies elsewhere?

OUR EDITORIAL STANDARD

What a useful case must show

We judge cases by how clearly they separate decisions, evidence and outcomes—not by the reputation of the organisation that published them.

01 · The decision and its economics

What had to be decided, what could have been done instead and what value was actually at stake?

02 · The mechanism and accountable owner

Which changes in people, process or technology produced the result, and who owned implementation?

03 · Evidence and what it cannot prove

What was measured against which baseline, how was impact attributed, and what remains only a claim?

Editorial rule. Our teaching cases are explicitly fictional. External case notes preserve links to the original accounts, and reported metrics are not represented as independently verified outcomes.

03 / METHOD

Seven questions each case answers

01 · What was the decision?

The choice within management's control, as distinct from the technology selected.

02 · What was the baseline?

Numbers, definition, population and period before intervention.

03 · What explained the gap?

Competing hypotheses and the evidence that distinguishes them.

04 · What alternatives existed?

Whether doing nothing, a simpler fix or a different tool would be better.

05 · How would it ship?

Owner, cadence, controls, dependencies and rollout sequence.

06 · What outcome counts?

Financial contribution, cost, risk, counterfactual and attribution.

07 · What would stop it?

Stop conditions agreed in advance, limitations, and what transfers to other settings.
