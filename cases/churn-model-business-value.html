CASE 02 · PREDICTIVE ANALYTICS

Contoso Play: is the better churn model worth deploying?

Contoso Play, a subscription video service, loses about 14.5% of its subscribers every 60 days. Its data science team proposes replacing a simple churn model with a more complex one. Ananya Rao, head of subscriber retention, must decide whether the new model justifies its cost, and how to find out whether contacting at-risk subscribers keeps any of them.

01 / THE SITUATION

A stronger model and an untested programme

Contoso Play offers Basic, Standard and Premium plans on monthly and annual contracts. In any 60-day period about 14.5% of its subscribers cancel. The retention team can contact a limited number of subscribers each cycle, with a payment reminder, a content recommendation or an offer to change plan.

Karthik Subramanian, who leads data science, had built two models on 10,000 subscriber records and tested them on 3,000 records held out from training. A logistic regression scored an AUC of 0.788; a gradient-boosting model scored 0.797. Subramanian recommended the gradient-boosting model. "It is the stronger model on every measure we track," he said.

Rao's concern lay elsewhere. Her budget was set in contacts, not in points of AUC, and the finance team had begun to ask what the retention programme returned. No campaign had yet been run against either model, so no one knew how many contacted subscribers would have stayed anyway, or how many would leave whatever they were offered.

Rao had to decide whether to adopt the new model, which subscribers to contact, and what evidence finance would need before the programme was expanded.

Decision owner

Head of subscriber retention.

Control functions

Model validation, data privacy and customer-contact controls.

Data available

3,000 held-out subscribers with known 60-day churn outcomes.

Data not available

The measured effect of any offer, incremental margin and reach.

02 / ANALYSIS

The new model ranks subscribers only marginally better, and neither model shows who can be retained

The gradient-boosting model's AUC of 0.797 is 0.009 higher than the baseline's 0.788, less than one percentage point. Without a confidence interval for the paired difference, the gain cannot be called significant. Both models concentrate risk well: the 10% of subscribers with the highest scores include 35.48% of all churners, a lift of 3.55 times, and the top 30% include 68.2%.

Predicting who will leave is a different problem from predicting who can be persuaded to stay. Some high-risk subscribers will cancel whatever they are offered, and some lower-risk subscribers may respond well. A churn model addresses only the first question.

03 / OPTIONS

Keep the simpler model until a pilot shows that the offers pay

OptionTrade-offAssessment

Use the logistic model with a randomised test of offersInterpretable, and easier to operate and explainRecommended for the pilot

04 / RECOMMENDATION

Make the economics of each offer the unit of evaluation

  1. 01 · SELECTSet eligibility, contact capacity and channel permissions. Score eligible subscribers, remove duplicates and suppress those who may not be contacted.
  2. 02 · RANDOMISEAssign comparable subscribers at random to treatment and control, holding back 20% of those selected as the control. Fix the allocation rule and the cohort before launch.
  3. 03 · TREATMake a documented offer through an approved channel, and record delivery, cost, acceptance, redemption and complaints.
  4. 04 · MEASURECompare retention, and contribution net of incentive and servicing cost, between treatment and control over a retention window set in advance.
  5. 05 · MONITORTrack complaints, fairness, consent, contact frequency, calibration and drift. A named manager has authority to pause the campaign.

05 / MEASURES

Whether the campaign pays depends on an effect that has not yet been measured

The calculator uses the churn rate in each decile of the holdout; the top three deciles average 32.89%. Selecting more subscribers extends the campaign into lower-risk deciles. The share of would-be churners who stay because of the offer is entered by the user. It is an assumption, not an observed result.

Exhibit 2 · Campaign contribution under stated assumptions

SCENARIO
Enter a scenario to calculate estimated net contribution.
Net contribution = expected churners among those selected × reach rate × share retained × (contribution − incentive) − subscribers selected × contact cost. The calculation assumes churn risk does not vary with reachability, and excludes taxes, fixed costs, discounting, differences in response between subscribers, and uncertainty.

The break-even retention effect equals the campaign's contact cost divided by (subscribers reached × their churn rate × net contribution per retained subscriber). Before the programme is expanded, the assumed effect should be replaced by the pilot estimate and its confidence interval.

06 / IMPLEMENTATION

A six-week randomised pilot

  1. WEEK 01The retention team agrees the definition of retention, the eligible population, the channel budget, contribution assumptions and guardrails. Data science checks the features for leakage.
  2. WEEK 02Risk and analytics validate the control-group sampling, model stability, fairness of targeting, contact permissions and scripts.
  3. WEEKS 03–04Campaign operations runs the randomisation, suppression, delivery and exception logging. An independent reviewer tracks complaints and other adverse outcomes.
  4. WEEKS 05–06The team evaluates the part of the retention window that has elapsed, and continues to measure until the window closes before declaring a result.

Rao should stop the roll-out if incremental contribution after treatment costs is zero or negative, if complaints exceed approved limits, or if the result is too uncertain to support deployment.
