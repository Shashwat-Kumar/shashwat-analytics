Microsoft's free Copilot now has usage limits that nobody, including your IT team, can see

From 9 November, larger commercial tenants met new fair-use limits on Copilot Chat for staff without a paid Microsoft 365 Copilot licence. The limits vary through the day, Microsoft does not publish them, and the limit message can offer an upgrade.

By Shashwat Kumar · 2026-11-10 · 4 min read · Analysis

Facts are attributed and linked to their sources. The analysis and opinions are the author's own.

What it is

On 3 November Microsoft told administrators in Message Center notice MC1536163 that it would apply fair-use limits to more Copilot Chat features for users without a Microsoft 365 Copilot licence in commercial tenants, starting 9 November. Image generation already had such limits. The limits apply on the web, desktop and mobile, "can vary throughout the day", and "Admins can't view or adjust the limits." Users who hit one see an in-product notice with a time to try again; responses already in progress are not interrupted. Microsoft's only recommended actions are to brief the help desk and review self-service licence requests.

Microsoft's support page adds that "Microsoft does not publish or share specific numeric limits" because service limits can vary. CRN reported that the limits began on 2 November for organisations with 2,000 or fewer seats and on 9 November for larger ones, that the limit notice in most organisations includes an option to request or buy a licence, and that partners can see which client users are reaching limits. It also quoted Microsoft saying that paid Microsoft 365 Copilot licences include fair-use limits of their own.

Why it matters

Many firms rolled out free Copilot Chat as their safe, approved AI tool, partly to keep staff away from unapproved ones. That plan assumed the approved tool would be there when people needed it. A limit that varies through the day and cannot be seen by the administrator is a capacity risk that operations teams cannot measure.

An AI tool your staff depend on, with a limit you cannot see, is a capacity risk nobody owns.

The practical effects are easy to predict. A staff member hits the limit in the middle of month-end work, is told to try later, and either waits, asks for a paid licence, or pastes the work into a personal tool. The last of those is the outcome security teams were trying to avoid. In my view firms should treat free AI chat as a best-effort service, not as part of any process with a deadline, and keep a record of who depends on it.

There is also a cost-control point. The limit notice is a sales channel: it can route users straight to a licence request or, if allowed, a purchase. Administrators can switch off direct purchases product by product. That setting deserves a check this week.

What I'd watch

  • Whether Microsoft gives administrators any usage visibility, even an alert when users approach a limit.
  • How often help desks see limit tickets in the first month, and whether staff move to unapproved tools.
  • How fair-use limits on paid Microsoft 365 Copilot licences are applied and disclosed.
