A model that knows when not to act.

In regulated finance, a mature AI system is judged as much by its restraint as by its capability. This console runs 20,000 synthetic bank decisions through a governance gate: stakes tiers, prohibitions, abstention when the data can't be trusted, and a breaker that suspends autonomy when results degrade. Change the controls and the whole stream re-runs in your browser.

A record nobody can quietly rewrite

Every verdict is written to a hash-chained ledger: each entry's SHA-256 covers its record and the entry before it. Edit one past decision and every hash after it stops matching.

Sealing the ledger

How the gate decides

Every proposal is checked in this order. The first rule that applies decides, so the most restrictive verdict always wins.

  1. Default deny. An action the policy does not name is blocked.
  2. Prohibitions. Blocked whatever the model believes: sanctions matches, do-not-contact.
  3. Abstention. Stale data, drift or out-of-distribution input: there is no sound basis to decide.
  4. Mandatory review. Vulnerable customers are decided by a person.
  5. Circuit breaker. Autonomy suspended while recent autonomous outcomes are degraded.
  6. Tier ceiling. Critical exposure is never decided autonomously.
  7. Confidence gate. Act only if confidence clears the bar for this tier and action.
