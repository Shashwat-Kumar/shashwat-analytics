CASE 03 · GOVERNANCE

Acme Learning: what to fix first

Acme Learning, an Indian education-technology company, has seven months before the core obligations of India's data-protection rules apply. An internal assessment rates the company 50% ready. Vikram Desai, chief operating officer, must decide what to fix first and how to report progress to the board.

RECOMMENDATION

Report exposure, not a percentage. Have counsel confirm which obligations apply, rank the verified gaps by exposure starting with children's data and the WhatsApp campaigns, give each gap an owner, and accept a gap as closed only on independent evidence.

01 / THE SITUATION

A readiness score and a deadline

Acme Learning Pvt Ltd sells online courses to school students and their parents, and runs a learning app used by the students themselves. Its personal data sits in five main processing activities: customer sign-up, WhatsApp marketing campaigns, payroll, the student learning app and customer support.

On 13 October 2026 the compliance team completed a readiness assessment against the Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 and the DPDP Rules 2025, whose core obligations apply from 13 May 2027. The assessment scored each activity, logged 23 gaps and labelled 10 of them priority one. Payroll scored 86 out of 100; WhatsApp marketing scored 23. Overall, the company was rated 50% ready.

Several findings were serious. None of the five activities had a breach-response playbook. The student app processed children's data without verifiable parental consent. The WhatsApp campaigns had no valid notice or consent, and no contract with the messaging vendor. Retention periods were undefined for four of the five activities.

The board had asked for a single readiness figure each quarter. Desai was wary of reporting 50% and watching the number climb. "If we fix the easy items first, the score goes up and our exposure on children's data stays where it is," he told the compliance lead. He had to decide how to order the remediation work, who would own it, and how the board should track progress.

Decision owner

Chief operating officer, with legal counsel and the data-protection lead.

Unit of assessment

Processing activity: purpose, systems, vendors and data life cycle.

Assessment

Five activities scored; 23 gaps logged, none yet independently verified.

Limitation

The 50% score comes from an internal rubric and has no standing as a legal compliance rating.

02 / ANALYSIS

The average conceals three activities that need immediate attention

The 50% figure combines a well-controlled payroll process with three weak customer-facing activities. The rubric's weighting is not documented, so the average cannot serve as evidence of compliance, and a high score in one activity cannot offset a material gap in another.

Scores out of 100 from Acme's internal rubric. They are not a legal assessment.

Four questions for each activity

  1. What personal data is collected, for what purpose, on what legal basis and under what notice?
  2. What happens when a person exercises a right, corrects their data or withdraws consent?
  3. Do retention, deletion and vendor-processing records exist, and do they match practice?
  4. Is access restricted, has incident escalation been rehearsed, and are control owners named?

Which provisions apply to each activity, from when, and with what exemptions, must be confirmed by counsel against the current official texts. The readiness score cannot settle those questions.

03 / OPTIONS

Prioritise by obligation and exposure rather than by score

ApproachRiskAssessment
Report the average scoreMaterial gaps are hiddenReject
Fix the easiest gaps first to raise the scoreThe score improves while exposure stays the sameReject
Rank gaps by mandatory obligation and exposure, with owners and evidenceRequires legal and operational judgementRecommended
Buy a privacy-management tool nowAutomates controls that are not yet definedDefer until requirements are settled

04 / RECOMMENDATION

Replace the score with a register that ties each gap to an obligation, an owner and evidence

Each entry in the register records the obligation and whether it applies, the processing activity, the failure, the records or people affected where known, severity, the control owner, the remediation owner, the due date, any compensating control, the test method, the evidence of closure and who approved any exception.

  1. 01 · VERIFYCounsel maps the relevant provisions to each processing activity and confirms commencement, exemptions and applicability.
  2. 02 · TRIAGEIdentify prohibited processing and material residual risk, starting with children's data and the WhatsApp campaigns. This work comes before anything that merely raises the score.
  3. 03 · REMEDIATESystem and business owners fix notices, consent flows, access, deletion, processor contracts and incident playbooks.
  4. 04 · TESTAn independent assurance team samples records and events to confirm that each control operates in practice as well as on paper.
  5. 05 · SUSTAINReview closures monthly, escalate overdue items and rehearse incident response.

05 / MEASURES

These are proposed measures; Acme does not yet report them. A control can exist without working, so both its design and its operation should be tested.

06 / IMPLEMENTATION

Close the most material exposures within twelve weeks

  1. WEEKS 01–02Privacy counsel confirms applicability and obligations. Data and IT confirm the processing inventory, processors and data flows. The risk team reassesses all 23 gaps rather than accepting the ten priority-one labels as given.
  2. WEEKS 03–06Operational owners fix the highest-risk verified gaps, with temporary mitigations approved by management and evidence requirements set in advance.
  3. WEEKS 07–10The assurance team tests remediation on live samples and rejects any closure that lacks adequate evidence.
  4. WEEKS 11–12The company rehearses a breach and a rights request, and reports open issues, residual exposure and exceptions to the board.

Acme should not describe itself as ready while a mandatory control is missing, a material breach path is unresolved, or the evidence for a closure cannot be reproduced.

07 / EVIDENCE

What the analysis supports, and what it does not

StatementBasis
Five activity scores, 23 gaps and ten priority-one labelsAcme's inventory and rubric
The 50% average demonstrates legal readinessNot supported
Named control owners and independent retestingRecommended, not yet in place
All obligations and dates have been legally validatedNot established; requires legal review

The recommendation to Desai is to manage remediation through the verified gap register, report exposure and closure to the board, and drop the aggregate readiness score as the headline measure.
