This week · updated 9 Nov 2026, 7 PM IST

  • Britain’s biggest banks are shutting outside AI agents out of customer accounts. The Telegraph reported on 8 November that HSBC, Nationwide, NatWest, Barclays and Lloyds have moved to block AI agents, in some cases specifically barring them from making transactions on a customer’s behalf without the customer’s direct involvement; the bans are described as pre-emptive, with no sign yet of bots getting into accounts, and the FCA is working with the industry on rules for agents in finance. It follows similar limits from US banks including JPMorgan, Citi and Schwab, and it shows where the line is settling: controlled, read-only access through approved open-banking services may survive, but an agent acting on the money still needs the customer, and nobody has yet answered who is liable when one gets it wrong. The Telegraph · Dealroom
  • GCash is piloting Gabby, an in-app assistant that completes a transaction rather than just answering questions. Reported 9 November, the pilot is open to selected users of the Philippine e-wallet; its first task is buying prepaid mobile load, where Gabby looks at past transactions to recommend an offer, then hands the request to GCash’s own cashier, and the user must confirm before any payment is made. Set beside the UK bans, it is the other half of the same pattern: financial apps are willing to run agents they built themselves, on their own payment rails and with a customer confirmation step, while keeping outside agents at the door. YugaTech
  • Deutsche Bank and Ant Digital Technologies published a joint white paper on agentic payments in wholesale finance on 9 November. It looks at agents in treasury and liquidity management, cross-border payment routing and trade finance, and argues for “bounded agentic execution” rather than open-ended autonomy, borrowing an IMF three-layer design that keeps the agent’s reasoning separate from a rule-based authorisation layer and from settlement itself. The safeguards it lists are the useful part for anyone drafting an agent policy: authority that is explicit, machine-readable, bounded and revocable, agent identity tied to an accountable owner, and “Know Your Agent” checks, so that an agent can propose a payment but cannot widen its own limits. A day after UK banks were reported shutting outside agents out, two large institutions are writing down what letting their own agents move money would take. PR Newswire
