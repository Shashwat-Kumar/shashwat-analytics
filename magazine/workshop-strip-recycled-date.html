Our news strip changed 16 times this week with no automated check. It held back stale stories, but let one back in under a newer date

Between 9 and 15 November the agent that writes The Signal's 'This week' strip merged 16 updates, each within 4 to 9 seconds and none with a status check. Its run notes show it rejected stories that were older than they looked. But a white paper first carried on 9 November came back on 13 November, dated by a later write-up.

By Shashwat Kumar · 2026-11-16 · 5 min read · Build note

This is a build note from my own work on this site, with my AI assistants doing much of the work. Every claim links to the commit, pull request or issue it comes from.

What we ran

Since 28 October an agent has had standing approval to refresh the "This week" strip on The Signal's front page about three times a day, with two or three sourced lines on the most notable AI developments of the last 48 hours. Between 2 PM IST on 9 November (#56) and 9 AM IST on 15 November (#75) it merged 16 updates. They included the Fed's Michelle Bowman on AI-related debt (#67), APRA's AI stress test and JPMorgan's stand against agent card spending (#71), BCG's bank governance benchmark (#73), ChatGPT Wallet (#74) and Vietnam's Circular 53 (#75).

The strip has become this issue's research feed. Four of its five news stories began as strip items. The fifth, Gemini 4 Hydrogen, was one the agent passed over on 14 November because it was not a finance story. For the strip that was the right call; for the magazine it was not, which is why the issue still needs a human editor.

What worked

It checked against the magazine. On 9 November (#56) it removed two items, on BMO and Cashfree, because Issue 06 already covered them.

It checked real dates. Run notes kept outside the repository show that at 7 PM on 14 November it rejected an NSE item and the Northern Trust–Microsoft onboarding agent because both dated from 9 November, and the strip had already carried the second one (#59). At 2 PM on 15 November it passed over the FCA's "agentic regulator" speech, which was getting fresh coverage this week; the speech itself was delivered on 19 October.

Run notes also show at least three runs, at 2 PM and 7 PM on 14 November and 2 PM on 15 November, that changed nothing because nothing new was strong enough.

What broke

A story came back under a newer date. Deutsche Bank and Ant Digital Technologies published their white paper Agentic Payments in Wholesale Finance on 9 November. The strip carried it that evening (#57), correctly dated, and dropped it on 10 November (#62). At 2 PM on 13 November it returned (#72) as a paper "reported on 12 November", citing Payment Expert's write-up from that day. The line was not false, and the write-up added real detail. But the 48-hour window is meant to be about when things happen, not when they are written about, and readers who saw the item on 9 November saw it again as new. It stayed up until 7 PM (#73).

Still no machine check. All 16 pull requests were merged between 4 and 9 seconds after they were opened, and none ran a status check. In Issue 06's build note, "None shipped", I said a check that can fail before publication was the one to build next. It has not been built.

The good decisions are still invisible. Every rejection described above is recorded only in run notes outside the repository. A reader of the site, or of the pull requests, cannot see them.

What we learned

Date the event, not the article. The agent's window rule worked when the only date available was the publisher's. It failed when a newer article described an older event. The check needs two dates, when something happened and when it was reported, and it should use the first.

The strip's own history is free memory. Every past version is in git. A check that fails a pull request when a linked document, or the same organisation and title, has appeared in the strip in the last 14 days would have caught the repeat. So would a check that every source date falls inside the window. Both are a few dozen lines of code.

The failure was not a false fact. It was an old fact presented as news, and that is harder to spot.

In finance this is a familiar failure in a new place. A customer-messaging agent that re-sends last week's offer under today's date, or a monitoring agent that raises an alert it has already raised, does no single thing wrong. It repeats itself with a newer timestamp. I am not putting a date on the check this time. The next build note will say whether it exists.
