CASE 01 · PERFORMANCE MANAGEMENT

Northwind Retail: one number for revenue

In October 2026, Meera Iyer, chief financial officer of Northwind Retail, must recommend how much the company spends on acquiring customers over the next six months. Revenue has grown 52% in a year, but each management report shows a different figure for it, and the cost of acquiring a customer has risen 27%.

RECOMMENDATION

Postpone the dashboard. Agree one definition for each core metric, reconcile the figures to the ledger, and approve acquisition spending on contribution rather than revenue. Northwind can complete this in 90 days with its existing systems.

01 / THE SITUATION

Five figures for the same month

Northwind Retail sells apparel and home products directly to consumers through its website and app. In the twelve months to September 2026 it recorded ₹55.28 crore of revenue from 356,385 orders, an average of ₹1,551 an order. Revenue in the second half of the year was 34.4% higher than in the first, even though the first half included the festive season.

The monthly business review had become a debate about whose numbers were right. E-commerce reported placed orders. Finance reported revenue net of returns and cancellations. Marketing reported revenue attributed to paid channels, and the app and warehouse teams each kept their own counts. By the September review, five versions of revenue were in circulation.

Rohan Mehta, head of growth, wanted to raise acquisition spending for the second half. He pointed to the repeat-purchase rate, which had risen from 57.1% in the first half to 64.0% in the second after the company launched a loyalty programme in May. "The customers we are buying are staying," he told the review. Iyer was less certain. Acquisition cost had risen from ₹429 to ₹546 per new customer, and no one could yet say what a customer contributed after discounts, returns and delivery.

The leadership team's first request was for a consolidated KPI dashboard. Before approving it, Iyer asked the business intelligence team to answer a narrower question: what would leadership need in order to trust the numbers on which the acquisition budget would be set?

Decision owner

Chief financial officer, with the head of growth.

Decision

Acquisition and retention spending for October 2026 to March 2027.

Data available

Monthly revenue, orders, average order value, acquisition cost and repeat rate, October 2025 to September 2026.

Data not available

Contribution margin, paid-channel attribution and cohort profitability.

02 / ANALYSIS

Revenue grew with order volume while the cost of each new customer rose 27%

Between October 2025 and September 2026, revenue and orders both rose 52.2%, and average order value ended the period where it began, at ₹1,670. The growth came from more orders, not larger baskets. Over the same period acquisition cost rose 27.3% and the repeat-purchase rate rose 12.5 percentage points. The comparison uses the first and last months only. Three explanations to test

  1. Volume. Order count accounts for the whole revenue increase; basket size was the same at both end-points.
  2. Acquisition quality. A higher acquisition cost may still be justified if the customers acquired contribute more over their lifetime. Northwind has no lifetime-value or channel-mix data with which to test this.
  3. Definitions. Repeat rate may be measured on a different customer base or look-back period from revenue. Until the definitions are aligned, the two series cannot be compared.

04 / RECOMMENDATION

Define, reconcile and report, then govern the forum that uses the numbers

  1. STEP 01Define. Agree and version one definition for each metric: revenue net of returns and cancellations, orders counted when paid, acquisition cost as channel spend divided by new paying customers, and repeat rate on a stated cohort and period.
  2. STEP 02Reconcile. Load daily order, payment, returns and advertising data into one reconciled table. Every exception has an owner and a documented waiver.
  3. STEP 03Report. Show revenue growth, acquisition cost, contribution after variable costs, repeat behaviour, channel mix and cohorts on one page, each with its source.
  4. STEP 04Govern. Finance approves margin figures and commercial leaders own the actions. Business intelligence is responsible for lineage, timeliness and open breaks; the business decision stays with the business.
A single source of truth, in this sense, is an agreed definition backed by a reconciliation process. It does not require one database or one report.

06 / IMPLEMENTATION

A 90-day plan with named owners

  1. DAYS 01–30Finance and Commercial agree the KPI definitions. Business intelligence documents source lineage and measures the current discrepancies.
  2. DAYS 31–60Data engineering builds the reconciled tables and tests them against at least three of the conflicting reports. Owners sign off the exceptions.
  3. DAYS 61–90Commercial runs the monthly review on the new pack, with channel and cohort economics. Finance reviews the contribution figures and the actions agreed.

Until acquisition-cost attribution, customer cohorts and variable contribution are reconciled, Northwind should not approve a large reallocation of media spending on the strength of the new reports.
