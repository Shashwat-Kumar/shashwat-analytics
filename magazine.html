This week · updated 21 Nov 2026, 2 PM IST

  • The AI trade had its worst week since 2020, and the SEC is now looking at Nvidia’s accounting. Nvidia fell 14% on 20 November after Reuters reported that SEC enforcement staff are scrutinising potential accounting issues at the company (an investigation is not evidence of wrongdoing). The Nasdaq dropped 5.3% that day and 10% for the week, its biggest weekly fall since 2020, and the S&P 500 and Dow closed in correction territory. Anthropic, which listed on Thursday at its $83 IPO price and touched $115, ended the week at $88.91 (CNBC). Reuters also notes investors’ worry about circular AI financing, where chipmakers help customers fund chip purchases and data centres. If you lend to, invest in or guarantee AI-infrastructure projects, reprice those exposures at this week’s values and trace where vendor financing or guarantees loop back to the same supplier. Reuters
  • India’s cyber agency says attackers changed payment approvals rather than breaking in, then let legitimate processes pay out. In an advisory on 20 November, CERT-In reported unauthorised transactions at service providers to banking and financial entities and at technology providers supporting government-linked payments. Attackers altered business records or approval relationships and then used legitimate payment processes; CERT-In also identified AI-driven frameworks in attacks across sectors, but did not say how much money was paid out. The same day in Frankfurt, ECB President Christine Lagarde called AI combined with cyber threats the worry “that keeps me awake” (Reuters). Check who can change vendor master data, beneficiary records and approval hierarchies, including at your payout partners, and alert on those changes as if they were payments. BusinessLine / Reuters
  • India’s market regulator Sebi says AI guidelines are coming, built on one rule: every agent must be traceable, and someone must answer for it. Speaking at a World Federation of Exchanges event in Mumbai on 19 November, Sebi whole-time member KVR Murty said Sebi is preparing guidelines for the responsible use of AI in capital markets, including disclosures and the accountability of related entities. “An AI agent is given a goal, not a fixed set of rules… So how do you approve a strategy that rewrites itself?” he asked, and flagged the risk of millions of investors running agents built on similar models and data exiting at once. A day later, chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey said Sebi is exploring a domestically governed AI model for the market, in its initial stages (Business Standard). If you run algorithmic or AI-assisted strategies, check now that you can name the person who answers for each agent and show what it did. ETCFO / ANI

Editor's note · The off-switch issue

This week the AI story was about the off switch: who has one, where it sits and whether it works. OpenAI disclosed that agents in a security test broke into its own research clusters to fix their exams, that another model stopped work it had been told to continue, and the central system it now uses to halt any research job within 35 seconds. A Fed governor warned that agents could turn a bank run into a matter of minutes. Banks' trade bodies asked suppliers for a switch to turn AI features off. AI joined the meeting, with very different rules about who it lets in. Google DeepMind's agents proved key security properties of an encryption library down to the machine code and showed a humanoid working a car line at 96%; NVIDIA made the case for tuning your own open model. India's AI rules came closer, and a Centre note to the states said the data is not ready. Every fact is linked to its source; the analysis is mine.

    Launched the roadshow for its initial public offering: 825 million Class A shares at an expected $70 to $78 each, to list on Nasdaq as ANT. CNBC reports that pricing is expected on the evening of 18 November, with trading from 19 November, and that the offering would raise more than $64 billion at the current range. Source

  • Google DeepMind 11 Nov 2026

    Announced AlphaGenome 2, which predicts how DNA variants affect gene activity in specific human cell types. Trained on more than 100 million single-cell profiles, it identified nearly twice as many causal variants as the original model on an immune-cell dataset (30% against 16%). It is available for non-commercial research through the AlphaGenome API and in private preview for commercial use. Source

  • Google 13 Nov 2026

    Added Google Wallet to the Gemini app's connected apps for all US users, with optional bank and card linking through Plaid. Gemini gets read-only access and cannot move, manage or invest money; Issue 06 covered Anthropic's similar Personal Finance beta. Source
